    ABOUT THE OBSERVATORY

    Structure, not stories.

    Event Horizon is an abstract map of connections between notes. Sectors are numbered, nodes use anonymous IDs, and no titles, text, paths, or semantic labels are displayed.

    Topology is not guaranteed anonymity. A distinctive pattern of connections can fingerprint a graph, even without its content.

    Every colored star in the disk is an actual note. All notes are included on desktop and mobile: sectors form interleaved spiral streams within one thin, three-dimensional accretion disk. Notes rotate inward and wrap individually to the outer edge, always outside the absorbing horizon. No note or connection is deleted, and no invented notes fill the disk.

    The dim background sky is separate decorative artwork. A bounded, camera-dependent ray approximation samples a disk-space image of the actual note stars, their finite light envelopes and short trails: far-side light bends into upper and lower arcs around the black shadow. Each flow-aligned envelope belongs to a real note; overlapping source light forms the luminous disk. Warped arcs are noninteractive repeated light, not extra notes. Inspect a direct star or use the complete anonymous ID selector. The source image also retains emission-weighted note height. A bounded shallow-depth correction and directional footprint filter reconstruct the warped streams; overlapping heights are still averaged. A soft geometric weighting reduces duplicate visible-source images and deliberately omits some secondary light. Brightness is artistically graded. This is not a full gravity simulation, exact physical lensing, or an energy-conserving transfer model. The narrow luminous images and modest highlights are emitted light, not a reflective surface. Source-independent procedural gas is disabled.

    Short colored trails are bounded exposures of those same notes along their actual visual trajectories, including in the frozen opening. Isolating a sector reveals its stream. Selecting a note adds a bounded spatial preview of its real connections, including cross-sector endpoints; the preview count reports submitted relationships, not visible line fragments. The paged connection list reaches every neighbor and separates within-stream and cross-sector counts. The notes-in-disk count reports included notes, not pixels currently visible through the horizon or viewport.

    Select the title to reveal the tools. Drag to orbit; scroll or pinch to approach. Follow a connection to visit its actual neighbor; Back restores the previous note, stream and connection-list context. Locate frames the selected note in the open scene. Home resets all streams and clears the exploration path. Free flight uses WASD, Q/E, or the touch thrust controls. An explicitly enabled flight pad stays available after closing the tools; End flight returns to orbit. Escape returns to the scene without clearing selection. Reduced motion starts paused, with all navigation still available.
