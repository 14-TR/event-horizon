ABOUT THE OBSERVATORY

Structure, not stories.

Event Horizon is an abstract map of connections between notes. Sectors are numbered, nodes use anonymous IDs, and no titles, text, paths, or semantic labels are displayed.

Topology is not guaranteed anonymity. A distinctive pattern of connections can fingerprint a graph, even without its content.

Every colored star in the disk is an actual note. All notes are included on desktop and mobile: sectors form interleaved spiral streams within one thin, three-dimensional accretion disk. Notes rotate inward and wrap individually to the outer edge, always outside the absorbing horizon. No note or connection is deleted, and no invented notes fill the disk.

The dim background sky is separate decorative artwork. A bounded, camera-dependent ray approximation now also samples a disk-space image of the actual note stars and their trails: far-side light bends into upper and lower arcs around the black shadow. Warped arcs are noninteractive repeated light, not extra notes. Inspect a direct star or use the complete anonymous ID selector. This thin-disk approximation flattens the small vertical spread; it is not a full gravity simulation or exact physical lensing. The narrow luminous images and modest highlights are emitted light, not a reflective surface. Procedural gas is disabled. Short colored trails are bounded exposures of those same notes along their actual visual trajectories, including in the frozen opening. Isolating a sector reveals only its stream and real within-sector connections. Cross-sector connections remain in the data and total neighbor counts. The bottom count reports notes included in the disk, not pixels currently visible through the horizon or camera.

In Orbit, drag to orbit and scroll or pinch to zoom. Select a sector to isolate its stream. In Fly, use WASD to move, Q/E for down/up, and drag to look; touch users can hold the six thrust buttons. Selecting a sector returns to Orbit. Reset view restores the home camera and all sectors. Cinematic view hides the HUD; its persistent exit button or Escape restores it. Render quality changes ray and trail detail, never note coverage. Escape otherwise clears a selection. Pause and reduced motion freeze stars and trails; deliberate camera controls still work.
